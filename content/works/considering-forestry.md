This dissertation considers what it means to model—and twin—complex systems such as forest environments. It argues that in designing and producing these models it is necessary to go beyond a focus on exactitude and precision.

Drawing methodological connections between closer-to-nature forestry and cybernetic planning, the thesis regards both as management approaches that aim to create conditions for system parts to govern themselves.
